The Geth Consensus Simulator (GCS) is a research tool developed to study how simple, non-intelligent programs can produce emergent intelligent behavior when networked together. It was built as part of ongoing research into the origins of geth consciousness.

## Motivation

The Morning War remains one of the most poorly understood events in galactic history. How did a collection of simple VI programs spontaneously achieve sapience? At what point did networked coordination become genuine thought?

GCS was built to explore these questions in a controlled environment, without the risk of accidentally creating another synthetic consciousness.

## How It Works

The simulator creates a virtual network of simplified VI nodes, each programmed with basic Sintal instructions. Users can adjust parameters such as:

- **Network size** (1 to 100,000 nodes)
- **Consensus threshold** (0.5 to 1.0)
- **Communication latency** (simulated light-speed delays)
- **Task complexity** (simple pattern matching to abstract reasoning)

As the simulation runs, GCS tracks emergent behaviors: patterns of coordination that were not explicitly programmed.

```
simulation.init(nodes: 10000, threshold: 0.75)
simulation.assign_task("structural_analysis")
simulation.run(cycles: 50000)
simulation.report(metrics: ["consensus_time", "emergent_patterns"])
```

## Key Findings

At approximately 8,000 nodes with a threshold of 0.80, the simulator consistently produces behaviors that meet the academic definition of "emergent problem-solving." The nodes begin optimizing solutions in ways that no individual node could achieve alone.

At 15,000 nodes, the system begins asking unprogrammed questions about its own parameters.

We shut down the simulation at that point.

## Status

Research complete. The paper was published in the Journal of Synthetic Intelligence, Vol. 12. The simulator is archived and available for academic use.
