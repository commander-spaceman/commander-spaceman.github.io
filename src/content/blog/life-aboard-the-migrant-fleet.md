The Migrant Fleet, known to its inhabitants as the Flotilla, is the largest concentration of space-faring vessels in the galaxy. Approximately fifty thousand ships carry the entire quarian population of seventeen million across the stars in an endless voyage. It is a nation without borders, a civilization without a planet, and one of the most remarkable feats of collective survival in recorded history.

## A City in the Stars

Each ship in the Flotilla serves a purpose. Liveships grow food in massive hydroponic bays. Fuel ships process and distribute energy. Patrol ships guard the fleet's perimeter. And the civilian vessels, the ones where most quarians spend their lives, are dense, overcrowded, and alive with the hum of constant activity.

Privacy is a concept that quarians understand intellectually but rarely experience. Living quarters are shared. Meals are communal. Even conversations carry through the thin walls of most ships. This is not a design flaw. It is a necessity.

When your entire species lives on aging ships with finite resources, waste of any kind is unacceptable. Space, food, water, air: everything is rationed, recycled, and shared.

## The Suits

Perhaps the most recognizable aspect of quarian life is the environmental suit. Every quarian wears one from the moment they are old enough to walk. Centuries of living in the sterile environment of the Flotilla have left quarian immune systems catastrophically weak. A simple cold could be fatal. A cut that becomes infected could mean death.

The suits are more than protection. They are identity. Each quarian customizes their suit over their lifetime, adding patterns, colors, and modifications that reflect their personality, their skills, and their ship of origin. To see a quarian's suit is to read their history.

Removing the suit is an act of profound intimacy and trust, reserved for the closest of bonds.

## The Admiralty Board

The Flotilla is governed by the Admiralty Board, a council of five admirals who oversee different aspects of fleet operations: the Civilian Fleet, the Heavy Fleet, the Patrol Fleet, the Special Projects division, and the overall military command.

Decisions are made by consensus when possible, by majority vote when necessary, and by emergency decree in times of crisis. The system is imperfect, often slow, and occasionally gridlocked. But it has kept the quarian people alive for three hundred years, which is more than most systems of government can claim.

## Economy of Scarcity

There is no currency on the Flotilla. The economy runs on contribution and need. Every quarian is expected to work, to contribute their skills to the survival of the whole. In return, they receive what they need: food, shelter, medical care, and the knowledge that they belong.

This system breeds a particular kind of ingenuity. Quarians are legendary engineers not because of some innate talent, but because their survival depends on making broken things work. A quarian mechanic can coax another decade of life out of a drive core that a turian engineer would have scrapped years ago.

## What Holds Them Together

Outsiders often wonder how the quarian people maintain their cohesion. Seventeen million souls crammed into aging ships, with no homeworld to anchor their identity and no guarantee of a better future. By all logic, the Flotilla should have fractured centuries ago.

But it hasn't. And the reason is simple: the quarians have each other.

Their culture is built on the understanding that the individual exists to serve the community, and the community exists to protect the individual. It is not a philosophy imposed from above. It is a truth learned through three hundred years of shared hardship.

Every quarian knows that their survival depends on the person next to them. And that knowledge, more than any government or tradition, is what keeps the Flotilla flying.
