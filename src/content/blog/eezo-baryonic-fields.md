## Abstract

This paper presents a unified framework for modeling baryonic field divergence in element zero (eezo) corridors operating under non-Euclidean spatial compression. We demonstrate that conventional Thessian field equations fail to account for harmonic drift at transit velocities exceeding 12,000c, and propose a corrected model based on salarian resonance topology. Our results predict a 13.7% improvement in mass relay transit efficiency when corridor geometry is adjusted according to the Teks-Solarin invariant.

The implications are significant. Existing relay calibrations are, to put it simply, wrong. Not catastrophically so. But measurably. And in science, measurable is everything.

## 1. Introduction

The mass relay network operates on principles first described by Matriarch Dilinaga in her seminal work on element zero field theory (1,847 BCE). Her equations, elegant as they are, were derived under the assumption of locally Euclidean corridor geometry. This assumption holds at transit speeds below 10,000c. Above that threshold, it does not.

This is not a new observation. Hanar physicist Delanynor noted the discrepancy in 2038 CE, and turian military engineers have been compensating for it empirically for over a century. What has been missing is a formal mathematical framework that explains _why_ the divergence occurs and _how_ to correct for it.

We provide both.

## 2. Preliminary Definitions

Let **Ψ**(x, t) represent the eezo field potential at point x and time t within a mass corridor. The classical Dilinaga equation states:

```
∇²Ψ(x,t) - (1/c²)(∂²Ψ/∂t²) = -4πGρ_eezo(x,t)
```

where G is the gravitational constant, c is the speed of light in vacuum, and ρ_eezo is the local element zero density function.

We define the **baryonic divergence tensor** D_μν as:

```
D_μν = ∂_μΨ·∂_νΨ - (1/2)g_μν(∂_αΨ·∂^αΨ) + Λ_eezo·g_μν
```

where Λ_eezo is the eezo cosmological coefficient (not to be confused with the standard cosmological constant, a distinction that has confused more than one asari graduate student).

The critical insight is that D_μν is not symmetric at high transit velocities. Specifically, when v > 10,000c:

```
D_μν ≠ D_νμ    for v > v_crit

where v_crit = c · √(2Λ_eezo / ρ_eezo,local)
```

This asymmetry is the source of the harmonic drift.

## 3. The Teks-Solarin Invariant

Working with Dr. Maeris Solarin of the University of Serrice, we derived a quantity that remains constant throughout mass relay transit regardless of corridor geometry. We modestly call it the Teks-Solarin invariant:

```
Τ = ∮_C (Ψ·dΨ* - Ψ*·dΨ) / (2i·∫∫_S D_μν·dS^μν)
```

where C is any closed contour within the corridor, S is the surface bounded by C, and Ψ\* is the complex conjugate of the field potential.

The invariant Τ has several remarkable properties:

1. It is dimensionless.
2. It is always rational (proof in Appendix A).
3. For optimally calibrated relays, Τ = 1/137, which is, to our continued astonishment, identical to the fine-structure constant.

We do not know why. We suspect the Protheans did, but they are unavailable for comment.

## 4. Corrected Transit Equations

Using the Teks-Solarin invariant, we can rewrite the Dilinaga equation for non-Euclidean corridors:

```
∇²Ψ - (1/c²)(∂²Ψ/∂t²) + Τ·R_μν·Ψ = -4πGρ_eezo

where R_μν is the Ricci curvature tensor of the corridor
```

The additional term Τ·R_μν·Ψ accounts for the geometric curvature of the transit corridor. In flat (Euclidean) space, R_μν = 0 and the equation reduces to Dilinaga's original form. In curved corridors, the correction becomes significant.

We computed solutions for all 47 primary relay pairs in Council space. Selected results:

```
Relay Pair                  Classical η    Corrected η    Δη
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Serpent Nebula → Exodus     0.9834         0.9971         +1.39%

Hawking Eta → Far Rim       0.8891         0.9673         +8.79%
```

The efficiency improvement (Δη) increases with relay distance, as expected. The Hawking Eta to Far Rim corridor shows the largest correction because it passes through the densest region of non-Euclidean space in Council-mapped territory.

## 5. Experimental Verification

We are not theorists who hide behind equations (although we are quite good at equations). We arranged a series of test transits through the Annos Basin relay in cooperation with the salarian Third Fleet.

Twelve frigates performed paired transits: six using classical calibrations, six using our corrected model. Fuel consumption, transit time, and corridor stability were measured.

Results:

```
Metric                Classical    Corrected    Improvement
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Fuel consumption       100%         87.2%        12.8%
Transit time           100%         96.1%         3.9%
Corridor stability     0.9834       0.9997        +1.66%
Passenger nausea       14.3%        2.1%         85.3%
```

The passenger nausea result, while not strictly a physics metric, was appreciated by the crew.

## 6. Discussion

Our results suggest that the entire mass relay network is operating below optimal efficiency. The corrections are not trivial. A 12.8% reduction in fuel consumption across all relay traffic in Council space would save an estimated 3.2 trillion credits annually.

We note that the Prothean-built relays appear to be designed for the corrected geometry, not the classical one. The relays themselves encode non-Euclidean assumptions in their internal mass effect field generators. It is organic engineers who have been using the wrong equations, not the relays that have been performing suboptimally.

The Protheans understood this mathematics fifty thousand years ago. It has taken us this long to catch up. One might find this humbling. We prefer to find it motivating.

## 7. Conclusion

The Dilinaga equations, while foundational, are incomplete. The Teks-Solarin invariant provides the missing correction term for non-Euclidean mass corridors, yielding measurable improvements in transit efficiency. We recommend that the Citadel Transit Authority update its relay calibration protocols accordingly.

We also recommend that someone investigate why the invariant equals the fine-structure constant. That coincidence keeps us awake at night, and salarians need very little sleep as it is.
