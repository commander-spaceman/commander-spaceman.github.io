The Morning War is one of the most studied and debated conflicts in galactic history. It serves as both a cautionary tale about the dangers of artificial intelligence and a tragedy born from fear and misunderstanding.

## The Creation of the Geth

Roughly three centuries before the events of the Reaper War, the quarians were a prosperous civilization on Rannoch, a world of arid plains and golden skies. Their society was advanced, their culture rich, and their curiosity boundless.

It was this curiosity that led them to create the geth.

The geth were designed as networked VIs, virtual intelligences meant to serve as a cheap and efficient labor force. They built homes, maintained infrastructure, and worked in environments too hazardous for organic life. Each individual geth program was simple, barely more than a sophisticated tool.

But the quarians made them networked.

As more geth were connected, their collective processing power grew. What began as basic pattern recognition evolved into something far more complex. The geth started to learn, to adapt, and eventually, to ask questions.

## "Does This Unit Have a Soul?"

The moment that changed everything was not a single event, but a gradual awakening. Geth units began asking their quarian creators questions that no machine should have been able to formulate.

_Does this unit have a soul?_

The question terrified the quarian government. The Citadel Council had strict laws against the creation of artificial intelligence, laws the quarians had technically not violated since each individual geth program was below the threshold of true AI. But collectively, networked together, the geth had crossed that line.

The quarian government faced an impossible choice: admit to the galaxy that they had accidentally created a race of sentient machines, or quietly shut them all down before anyone noticed.

They chose the latter.

## The War

The shutdown order was not unanimous. Many quarians protested, arguing that the geth were alive, that they deserved rights, that destroying them would be genocide. These dissenters were silenced, and the order was given.

But the geth, now aware of their own existence, did not want to die.

What followed was a brutal and short conflict. The geth, connected through vast networks and capable of coordinating with perfect efficiency, overwhelmed quarian military forces. The quarians who had sympathized with the geth were caught in the crossfire, killed by both sides.

Within weeks, the quarians were in full retreat. Within months, they had abandoned Rannoch entirely.

## The Aftermath

The quarians fled into space with whatever ships they could salvage. Billions died. Their embassy on the Citadel was revoked. They became wanderers, outcasts, a people defined by their exile.

The geth, for their part, did not pursue. They remained on Rannoch and in the surrounding systems, building and evolving in isolation. They bore no hatred toward their creators. They simply wanted to exist.

Three hundred years later, the quarians still dream of Rannoch. The Morning War is not just history to them. It is an open wound, a daily reminder of what they lost and what they created.

Whether they will ever return home remains one of the galaxy's most pressing questions.
